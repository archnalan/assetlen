# David — The Contractor

*Builds Peter's projects. On site, on the road, or on the phone. Never at a desk.*

---

## Who he is

David runs the build. He has several crews working concurrently — plastering, conduiting,
undercoat — sometimes on the same day, sometimes on the same floor. He may have a clerk of
works capturing site activity when he is not there.

He is capable and trusted but not formal in his process. Drawings live in his email and
his WhatsApp. Variations are agreed on the phone. Peter's preferences are in his head. He
knows what Peter cares about and filters accordingly, by instinct, every evening.

**David is the adoption risk.** If Assetlen costs him more effort than WhatsApp, it dies —
regardless of how much Peter likes it. Everything else in this document follows from that.

---

## What his day actually looks like

- Three crews, three activities, one site.
- He is not there for part of it.
- The clerk of works has photos on his phone that nobody has organised.
- By evening David is expected to tell Peter what happened, in a way Peter will read.
- He is doing this on a phone, tired, often travelling.

Today he is a **human relay**: he receives site material in one place and re-types,
re-sends and re-explains it in another.

---

## His problems, in his words

- *"I'm the middleman between my clerk and my client and it's an hour of my evening."*
- *"If I add the clerk to the group he posts things the client shouldn't see raw."*
- *"I built to a drawing that turned out to be superseded."*
- *"Peter says he never chose that colour. I know he did. I can't find where."*
- *"I've done the work, I'm waiting on the money, and we're going back and forth on the phone."*
- *"Peter decides things too late and then wonders why we're waiting on stone."*

---

## The selfish payoffs (why he stays)

Assetlen must earn its place in David's day with things he wants for himself, not things
that are good for the client.

| Payoff | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Gets paid faster** | Claims carry their own evidence. Peter approves without a phone call. |
| **Covered on decisions** | Peter's choices are written down, attributed and timestamped. No more "I never said that." |
| **Stops being a relay** | The clerk captures. The system drafts. David approves in three minutes. |
| **Builds to the right drawing** | Current revision is always the one on top. Superseded revisions are archived, not deleted. |
| **A backlog he can price early** | Parked items and long-lead materials surface before the stage opens, so he can order while still finishing plaster. |
| **A defensible record** | When a wall cracks in November, he has who-did-what-on-which-day without reconstructing it from memory. |

---

## Capture: it must be dumber than WhatsApp

The clerk of works opens the app, sees today's active deliverables, taps one, shoots.

- No captions required.
- No forms.
- No judgement about what the client wants.
- Three taps, maximum.

Concurrent crews simply mean three destinations instead of one. Everything lands in the
**site log** — David's complete operational record, never sanitised, never seen by Peter.

Whatever intelligence Assetlen has must run *after* capture, never during it.

---

## Curation: David edits, he does not author

By late afternoon the system has drafted the client brief, **grouped by deliverable, not by
time** — plastering, conduiting, undercoat as three blocks, each with its best two or three
frames and a progress line. Chronology is what makes WhatsApp unreadable.

David opens the draft on the train:

- Swipe to drop a photo.
- Tap to promote one the draft missed.
- One voice note per block becomes the narrative line.

Three minutes. He is editing a draft, not writing a report — the difference between a habit
and something abandoned in week two.

**If he does nothing, it publishes anyway at the cutoff.** If David's silence produces
silence, Peter is chasing him on WhatsApp again by Thursday.

---

## The line David cannot curate

David controls **emphasis**. He does not control **truth**. Four things bypass curation and
reach Peter regardless:

1. Anything that moves money.
2. Anything that moves a date.
3. Anything that changes an agreed spec.
4. Blockers, and decisions Peter owes.

This boundary is what makes Peter trust a filtered feed. It must be stated to both parties
once, in plain language, and never quietly widened.

---

## Confirmation fatigue — the thing that will corrupt the record

Assetlen reads what David and the clerk post and proposes structure: *Material: 20mm
porcelain, matte. Price £X/m². Agreed 4 Aug.* One tap to confirm.

If it asks ten times a day, David taps through blindly and the register becomes
confidently wrong — worse than no register at all.

> **Extract only on money, materials, dates and decisions. Ignore everything else.**

---

## Failure conditions

Assetlen has failed David if:

- Posting a photo takes longer than it does in WhatsApp.
- He is confirming more than a handful of extractions a day.
- He has to be present for Peter to get an update.
- Notifications are slower or less reliable than WhatsApp's.
- There is nowhere for informal talk, so the WhatsApp group survives — and once it
  survives, receipts start landing in it again.

---

## The one sentence

**David wants to stop being the relay between his site and his client — without filling in
a single form.**
