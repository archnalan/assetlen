# Nalan — The Architect-Contractor

*Designs Peter's projects and builds them. On site, on the road, or on the phone. Never at a desk.*

> Grounded in [whatsapp-evidence.md](whatsapp-evidence.md) — the HOUSE PLAN thread,
> 18 Sep 2025 → 4 Aug 2026, 1,529 messages. Line citations below refer to that export.
> Previously named "David" in these documents.

---

## Who he is

Nalan is not only the builder. He is the **architect, the contractor, and the
statutory agent** in one person. On HOUSE PLAN he personally:

- produced and revised the architectural drawings, the door/window schedule and the
  wardrobe schedule;
- drove the approvals — physical planner, local-council letter, land title copy, the
  district town council's site inspection, the building control committee sitting;
- assembled and twice revised the BoQ with a consulting engineer, stripping 10% contingency
  and ~40M UGX of cost out of it;
- specified materials down to brand and grade (*"don't procure brand A, we need at least
  brand B; cement must be CEM II; 6-inch drainage pipe heavy duty; aggregate
  machine-crushed, not hand-crushed; lake sand from the usual pit"*);
- ran three concurrent workstreams — **main house**, **guest wing**, **external works** —
  plus a separate office building for the same client in another town;
- and posted every single site photo himself.

This matters more than it sounds. **Roughly half the commitment traffic on this project
is design, not construction** — a washroom moved, a door returned to the closet, balconies
deleted, an entire extra floor added. Every one of those carries a cost delta and a
knock-on to a schedule. A tool that models only build progress models half of Nalan's job.

**Nalan is still the adoption risk.** If Assetlen costs him more effort than WhatsApp, it
dies — regardless of how much Peter likes it. Everything else here follows from that.

## What his day actually looks like

The export is unambiguous about the rhythm:

- Site activity all day. He is frequently *not* there — at the client's other site, in
  transit, at church, at a supplier, at the planner's office.
- **He posts at night.** 12:56 AM, 11:39 PM, 10:39 PM, 11:15 PM, 1:31 AM. The dominant
  pattern is a **batch dump from the camera roll after the day is over** — 17 photos at
  7:40 PM on 25 May, 18 at 9:56 PM on 16 July, 16 at 12:56 AM on 4 May, 13 at 11:15 PM
  on 3 Dec.
- There is **no clerk of works in this thread.** The relay is Nalan himself, and the second
  pair of eyes on site is the client's own co-principal (see [Dinah.md](Dinah.md)).
- Roughly one message in twelve is a *"good morning team"* or a *"thank you for the
  continued support"* — relationship maintenance he does deliberately and will not give up.

> **Design implication.** P4's "open the app, tap today's deliverable, shoot" describes
> in-the-moment capture. Nalan's actual behaviour is **retrospective batch import**. If the
> camera-roll multi-select path is worse than WhatsApp's, the app is dead on arrival —
> the share-sheet target is not a P9 nicety, it is Nalan's primary capture route.

that are good for the client.

## Capture: it must be dumber than WhatsApp

The person capturing opens the app, sees today's active deliverables, taps one, shoots.

- No captions required.
- No forms.
- No judgement about what the client wants.
- Three taps, maximum.
- **And a bulk path**: select 17 photos from the camera roll, assign them to one or two
  deliverables in a single pass. This is the route Nalan actually uses.

Concurrent workstreams simply mean three destinations instead of one — main house, guest
wing, external works ran simultaneously for five months. Everything lands in the
**Site Log**: Nalan's complete operational record, never sanitised, never seen by Peter.

Whatever intelligence Assetlen has must run *after* capture, never during it.

## Curation: Nalan edits, he does not author

By late afternoon the system has drafted the client brief, **grouped by deliverable, not by
time** — main house, guest wing, retaining wall as three blocks, each with its best two or
three frames and a progress line. Chronology is what makes WhatsApp unreadable.

The proof is in the thread. On 25 Feb Nalan posted **17 photos in one minute**. Peter's
reply, two hours later: *"Nothing much changed."* Seventeen frames in posting order
communicated less than one same-vantage-point pair would have.

Nalan opens the draft in the evening:

- Swipe to drop a photo.
- Tap to promote one the draft missed.
- One voice note per block becomes the narrative line.

Three minutes. He is editing a draft, not writing a report — the difference between a habit
and something abandoned in week two.

**If he does nothing, it publishes anyway at the cutoff.** The thread proves why: silence
from Nalan produced *"How is today's progress"* from Peter within 24–48 hours, seventeen
separate times.

## The line Nalan cannot curate

Nalan controls **emphasis**. He does not control **truth**. Four things bypass curation and
reach Peter regardless:

1. Anything that moves money.
2. Anything that moves a date.
3. Anything that changes an agreed spec.
4. Blockers, and decisions Peter owes.

This boundary is what makes Peter trust a filtered feed. It must be stated to both parties
once, in plain language, and never quietly widened.

## Confirmation fatigue — the thing that will corrupt the record

Assetlen reads what Nalan posts and proposes structure: *Material: Tororo CEM II, 150 bags,
for the excavated retaining-wall section. Agreed 13 Jun.* One tap to confirm.

The thread shows the trigger is narrow and the yield is high. A single message on 11 June
carries five distinct material commitments; a single message on 13 June carries four
quantities. Meanwhile hundreds of messages are *"Okay"*, *"Noted"*, *"Good progress"* —
which must produce nothing at all.

If it asks ten times a day, Nalan taps through blindly and the register becomes
confidently wrong — worse than no register at all.

> **Extract only on money, materials, dates and decisions. Ignore everything else.**

---

## Failure conditions

Assetlen has failed Nalan if:

- Posting a photo — or a batch of seventeen — takes longer than it does in WhatsApp.
- He is confirming more than a handful of extractions a day.
- He has to be present for Peter to get an update.
- Notifications are slower or less reliable than WhatsApp's.
- A decision made on a phone call still has nowhere to land in ten seconds.
- There is nowhere for informal talk, so the WhatsApp group survives — and once it
  survives, receipts start landing in it again.
