# Peter — The Developer

*Property developer. Several projects live at once. Rarely on site.*

---

## Who he is

Peter funds and directs residential development. At any moment he has two or three
projects running at different stages. He is not a construction professional and does not
want to become one. He runs his projects on trust, a verbal agreement, and a phone.

His arrangement with David is deliberately informal: they agree a stage, Peter funds it
up front, and David builds it. There is no quantity surveyor, no contract administrator,
no monthly valuation meeting. Peter does not want that machinery. He wants to know that
the stage he paid for is being built the way they agreed.

Today that knowledge arrives through a WhatsApp group.

---

## What he is actually doing when he opens his phone

Peter is not browsing photos. He is doing one of four things, and all four are the same
underlying act:

1. Looking for a **promise document** — the quote, the receipt, the invoice he already cleared.
2. Looking for a **photo that proves a promise** — what the wall looked like when they agreed it.
3. Looking for a **material that was agreed** — the tile, the finish, the spec.
4. Wanting to **sketch on top of an image and ask a question** — circle the thing, ask why.

In each case he is holding a past commitment up against present reality.

> **Peter does not need a message archive. He needs a commitments register.**

WhatsApp stores messages. Messages have positions in a stream, and positions expire the
moment new messages arrive. So Peter has no way to point at anything — he can only find it
again and re-send it. That is why the same receipt appears five times in the thread.

---

## The daily loop he wants

- Wakes up somewhere else.
- Opens one page. Reads it in under a minute.
- Sees what moved yesterday, what is blocked, what he owes a decision on.
- Occasionally opens a photo, circles something, asks a question.
- Closes the phone.

He does not want to request the update. He does not want to chase David. The update
should arrive whether or not anyone remembered to send it.

---

## What Peter values in an MVP

| Need | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **A glance, not a scroll** | One daily brief, organised by the work he is paying for — not forty photos in posting order. |
| **Evidence he can trust** | Camera-captured, timestamped, same vantage point over time. Day 1 / Day 8 / Day 15 of the same corner is worth more than 40 random shots. |
| **A decisions queue** | "Tile choice needed by Thursday or the screed stops." One list, with consequences attached. This is his real anxiety — not photos. |
| **Money against progress** | Stage funded £X, claimed to date £Y, evidence attached. Nothing more sophisticated. |
| **Findability months later** | "What did I approve on the balustrade?" answered in one search, including things that were only ever pictures. |
| **The right to reopen** | A cleared item can still be questioned without it feeling like an accusation. |

---

## What frustrates him today

- **Endless repeated content.** He re-sends the receipt because there is nowhere to point.
  Every re-send creates a second copy, which future searches also find. The mess compounds.
- **Screenshots and markup that orphan themselves.** He circles a line on a screenshot; the
  markup is now a new image with no connection to the original document.
- **Images are unsearchable.** The reason he scrolls is that WhatsApp cannot read a receipt.
- **Answers dissolve.** David answers the question, but the answer is a message, so next
  month it is the next thing Peter has to scroll for.
- **Ideas die.** He saw a boundary wall design on Pinterest in June. External works start in
  September. Nobody remembers, and the picture is 800 messages back.
- **One volume dial.** Either he drowns in conduit photos or he is in the dark. There is no
  middle setting.

---

## Peter's trust condition

Peter will accept a filtered feed — he does not want everything — **but only if he is told
the rule.** Say it once, plainly:

> *You see the things that matter: money, dates, specs, blockers, decisions you owe, and
> evidence on stages you funded. You do not see the ordinary volume of site life. David
> controls emphasis. He does not control those facts.*

A curated feed he knows is curated beats a raw feed he would never read.

---

## Failure conditions

Assetlen has failed Peter if:

- The brief does not arrive when David is busy or absent.
- He has to go somewhere to find decisions he owes.
- He asks a question and cannot tell, later, whether it was answered.
- A parked idea resurfaces too late to act on.
- Anything requires more taps than sending a photo in WhatsApp.

---

## The one sentence

**Peter wants to hold a past commitment up against present reality — in seconds, from
anywhere, without asking anyone.**
